Savage Grappling · social media review · 27 Sep 2026

The events doubled.
The page didn't.

Savage Grappling went from 271 athletes at the Chelsea comp in April 2023 to 556 at the Hungarian Community Centre in June 2026. The business is working. The social media is still posting like a first-year local comp, so it isn't bringing in new competitors, new gyms or sponsors at the rate the events deserve.

This page has the diagnosis from the 27 videos and screenshots you sent, a day-by-day plan for the 4 October event starting today, and every banner, post and reel I designed.

Why the page is stuck

I watched every clip frame by frame. The footage isn't the problem: the cinematic highlight has takedowns, slams, screaming crowds, arm raises and a woman fist-pumping after a win. It's how the clips are put together.

What the first second showsVideosWhat the viewer does
Logo, title card or event banner14 of 22Scrolls. The platform reads that as "boring" and stops showing it.
Empty hall, mats being laid, truck being loaded5 of 22Scrolls. No people, no action, 30–130 seconds long.
Photo slideshow or static photo2 of 22Maybe watches if they're tagged in it.
Wide crowd shot with no action yet1 of 22Doesn't know what they're watching.
A match, a takedown or a crowd reaction0 of 22This is what stops the scroll, and it never opens a video.

Counts are the first frame of each unique video (repeat uploads removed). Most videos are 30–80 seconds long. Short-form videos that grow accounts are usually 7–15 seconds.

No hook

TikTok and Reels decide in about 1.5 seconds whether to show a video to more people. Almost every Savage video spends those seconds on the logo fading in.

The action is buried

The best moments are 1–2 second shots inside 77-second montages. Each takedown should be its own 8-second post.

It's about the event, not the athletes

People share videos of themselves. 500 athletes per event means 500 people who'd repost a clip of their own match, and their gym would too.

Every post asks for money

"Sign up now" to people who don't follow you yet is a cold ask. Pages grow on entertainment first: 4 fun posts earn 1 sign-up post.

Talking heads with no cuts

One shot against a wall for 45–60 seconds. The information is good (codes, venue, para division), but it has to be cut to under 20 seconds with the offer first.

Nothing worth saving

No carousels, so nothing gets saved or sent to a team-mate. "First comp? What to expect" is the most saveable thing a comp can post.

Wrong shape

Several videos were filmed landscape or square and posted into a vertical feed, so the action is small in the middle with black bars around it.

It looks small

Nothing on the page says "556 athletes" or "20+ events". Gyms, sponsors and first-timers judge size from the page, and right now it undersells the event.

How pages that grow do it

The big grappling pages (Grappling Industries, which runs the same round-robin format in over 200 events a year, and ADCC Australia at about 6k Instagram followers) follow the same pattern. I couldn't pull their live view counts from here, so this is the pattern, not their numbers:

Savage now

  • Opens on the logo
  • 60–80 second montages
  • Rarely tags athletes or gyms in videos
  • Same edit posted everywhere
  • Posts cluster around sign-up deadlines
  • No carousels

Pages that grow

  • Opens on the most violent or emotional second
  • One moment per clip, 7–15 seconds
  • Tags both athletes and both academies, every time
  • Posts clips for weeks after each event
  • Results posts that tag every podium gym
  • Saveable carousels: rules, what to expect, results
Stick these on the wall

The new rules

  1. The first second is the best second

    Open on the takedown, the slam, the tap or the scream. The logo goes at the end, or nowhere, since the profile picture already shows it.

  2. One moment per video, 7–15 seconds

    A 77-second highlight is ten separate posts. Cut it up. Short videos get replayed, and replays push them to more people.

  3. Tag every athlete and both academies

    Put "@athlete vs @athlete · @gym vs @gym" in the caption and a text tag on screen. Use Instagram's Collab invite with the winning gym so the post appears on their page too.

  4. Film vertical, film close

    Hold the phone upright at mat level, 2–3 metres from the action. One phone per 2 mats on event day.

  5. 50 / 30 / 20 content mix

    Half action clips, 30% people and stories (first-timers, kids, the para division, coaches), 20% sign-up posts. The action earns the attention the sign-up posts spend.

  6. Post for 4 weeks after every event

    Each event gives you 30+ clips. Post one a day while the athletes still care. That keeps the page active between comps.

  7. Show the size

    Say "556 athletes", "20+ events" and "3–4 matches guaranteed" in banners, bios and captions. Size is proof, and it's already true.

Local first, then Australia-wide

How to grow bigger

Gyms are the customer

Gym ambassador codes

Give each academy its own discount code (like the OCT2026 and JULY26 codes already in use), such as BAYSIDE10. The coach gets a free entry or a merch pack for every 10 sign-ups.

The codes also show which gyms bring people, so you know who to thank and who to chase.

Already built

Make Best Overall Academy a season

Keep a running points table across the year and post it after every event. Gyms that are 2nd will bring more students to the next one to catch up. That's repeat entries every event.

Free reach

Instagram Collab posts

Invite the winning gym and the tagged athletes as Collaborators on results and clip posts. The post then shows on their profile and to their followers, and it counts toward yours.

Paid, small

$15–20 a day, 10 days before each event

Boost the best-performing reel on Instagram and Facebook to people within 30 km of the venue who are interested in BJJ, MMA or grappling. Then run a second ad to people who watched 50%+ of a reel, with the entry link. That's about $200 per event, or around 3 extra adult entries to break even.

Press

The para division is news

Local papers, community radio and council newsletters cover inclusive sport. One article with the venue name gets you in front of parents and new gyms who don't follow BJJ pages.

Australia-wide

Sydney and Brisbane with a host gym

You've already run Sydney and Brisbane. For each interstate event, partner with one big local academy as the "host gym". They get a free team entry and co-branding, and they fill the first 100 spots. Build 4 weeks of that city's clips before announcing.

Look bigger

Livestream Mat 1

A phone on a tripod streaming the main mat to Facebook and YouTube lets families watch from home, gives sponsors something to point to, and fills YouTube with Savage matches.

Fix February

New Year, first comp

February was the smallest recent event (299). Open early-bird entries in December with a "New Year, first comp" push to white belts, since January is when new members join gyms.
