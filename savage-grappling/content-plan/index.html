The 80 / 20 rule

About 80% of posts are real footage and people. At most 20% are designed graphics (dates, entries, results). Over 6 months that's roughly 430 real posts and 110 designed ones, and the calendar below keeps to it.

Batch it

Sunday night: cut the week's 7 midday clips and 7 evening videos in one sitting (CapCut or Instagram's editor). Mornings are memes and polls you can make on your phone in a few minutes.

Footage supply

One event gives about 50–100 usable moments if 2–3 people film vertically on the day. After every event, ask athletes to send their own clips with one line about the match. That alone fills the midday slot for 6 weeks.

What each post looks like

Post designs

Simple and raw beats polished. The only design rule that matters: one line of big, bold text in the top third, and the action already happening in the first second.

Other people's videos

Using other people's clips the right way

Reposting viral BJJ clips is how big pages grow, but downloading someone's video and posting it as your own can get posts removed and accounts struck for copyright. Do it like this instead:

Do

Stitch, Duet, Remix

On TikTok and Instagram, use the built-in Stitch, Duet or Remix on public videos that allow it. The original creator is credited automatically. Add Savage's take: "This happens at Savage every comp" + your own clip.

Do

Ask, then repost with credit

DM the creator: "Hey! Love this clip. Can we repost it on Savage Grappling with full credit and a tag?" Keep the screenshot of their yes. Tag them in the first line of the caption and as a Collab.

Do

Athletes' own Savage clips

Anything filmed at Savage by a parent or teammate: ask them to send it or invite you as a Collaborator. Most will say yes, and their friends see it on both profiles.

Don't

Don't use paid media footage

FloGrappling, UFC Fight Pass, ADCC and similar broadcast footage is owned by them. Stitch their public posts if allowed, but never download and re-upload.

Comments drive reach

The comment routine

1. End every caption with a specific question

"Who won this?" beats "Thoughts?". "What would you have done from here?" beats "Comment below".

2. Reply to the first 20 comments within an hour

Early replies double the comment count and tell the platform the post is active.

3. Pin the funniest comment

It sets the tone and gets people joining in.

4. Turn the top comment into the next video

Use "reply with video". Arguments about a ref call can run for three posts.

5. Ask for tags, not likes

"Tag the teammate who'd do this" spreads the post to people who don't follow you yet.

6. Keep it friendly

Debate the call, never the person. Delete abuse, and never post a clip that mocks a kid or an injured athlete.

Stop doing these

What not to do

  • No logo introsThe first second must be action or a face. The profile picture already shows the logo.
  • No montages over 20 secondsOne moment per post. A 77-second highlight is ten posts.
  • No "sign up now" every dayAt most 1 post in 5 asks for money. Entertainment earns the right to sell.
  • No sponsor band on every postIt makes everything look like an ad. Sponsors get a monthly spotlight and a mention in event captions.
  • No empty halls or truck-loading at normal speedBehind-the-scenes only works sped up to 10–15 seconds with a hook.
  • No landscape or square videoVertical 9:16, filmed close at mat level.
  • No posting without tagsEvery clip names and tags both athletes and both gyms, and uses Collab.
  • No reposting others' videos without permissionUse Stitch, Duet or Remix, or ask first.
  • No clips of injuries for viewsInjury clips go viral, but they hurt trust with parents and gyms. Skip them.
  • No mocking kids or beginnersCelebrate effort. Memes punch at situations, not people.
  • No 30 hashtags3–5 relevant ones. Location tag every time.
  • No disappearing between events3 posts a day, every day, including the quiet weeks.
